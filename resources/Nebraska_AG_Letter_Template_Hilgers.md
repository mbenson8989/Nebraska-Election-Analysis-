**Re: Request for a statewide investigation of county refusals to provide cast vote records, and for the State to demonstrate the integrity of the November 3, 2026 general election, under the Guarantee Clause, U.S. Const. art. IV, § 4**

Dear Attorney General Hilgers:

## 1. The Guarantee Clause

"The United States shall guarantee to every State in this Union a Republican Form of Government." U.S. Const. art. IV, § 4. A republican form of government depends on elections the public can check. I write as a registered Nebraska voter to ask your office to act on that duty.

## 2. Counties are refusing to provide cast vote records

Counties across Nebraska are refusing to follow the public-records law and are refusing to provide cast vote records (CVRs) when they are requested. A CVR is the electronic record of how a tabulation system recorded the votes on each ballot. It does not identify the voter.

## 3. No Nebraska statute names or expressly closes a CVR

I have not found any Nebraska statute that uses the words "cast vote record" or "CVR," or that expressly prohibits public access to such a record. The statutes being relied on do not say what a refusal of a CVR would require them to say.

**Public records include computer files.** Except when another statute "expressly provides that particular information or records shall not be made public," Nebraska law provides that "public records shall include all records and documents, regardless of physical form, of or belonging to this state, any county," or other listed public body. It adds: "Data which is a public record in its original form shall remain a public record when maintained in computer files." Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712.01(1).

**The exemption list does not include a CVR.** Section 84-712.05 lists the records that "may be withheld from the public." Subsections (1) through (30) of that section do not mention cast vote records, ballot images, or electronic tabulation records. The only use of the word "elections" in the list is "records of elections filed under section 44-2821," which concerns professional-liability insurance filings, not the tabulation of an election. Neb. Rev. Stat. § 84-712.05. Section 84-712.04 requires a written denial to cite "the particular statute and subsection thereof" relied on. Section 84-712.08 is a limited exception tied to federal funds, services, or essential information. Neither section creates an exemption for a CVR.

**Section 32-1031 requires preservation of a record of the results.** After the canvass, "the county canvassing board shall provide a record of the results to the election commissioner or county clerk either in a ledger or by using a computer printout. The election commissioner or county clerk shall preserve the record of the results" for the period set by the State Records Administrator. Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-1031(3). That section does not use the term "cast vote record." A ledger or a computer printout of canvass results is not, by the statute's own words, a ballot-level CVR. I submit, as an argument, that a CVR is the electronic record of the same tabulation those official results summarize, and that § 32-1031 at a minimum requires the preserved "record of the results" to be kept.

**Section 32-1032 limits inspection of some election materials, but it does not name a CVR.** This is the Election Act provision that has been used to restrict access to election materials. It requires county election officials to keep election materials, including ballots-cast containers and the official summary of votes cast, for not less than twenty-two months when the election involves federal offices. It then states: "The precinct sign-in register, the record of early voters, and the official summary of votes cast shall be subject to the inspection of any person who may wish to examine the same after the primary, general, or special election." It also states that the election commissioner or county clerk "shall not allow any other election materials to be inspected, including ballots, the names of voters who filled out a provisional voter identification verification envelope pursuant to section 32-915.03, and provisional ballot envelopes, except when an election is contested or the materials become necessary to be used in evidence in the courts." Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-1032.

Section 32-1032 does not mention CVRs. It expressly opens the official summary of votes cast to public inspection. A CVR names no voter. I submit that a nameless electronic record of how a scanner tabulated each ballot is not a "ballot" and is not closed by this section. If a custodian or the Secretary of State is citing § 32-1032, or any other section, as a bar to a CVR, I ask your office to say whether that reading is correct. The citation I have been given, if any, is: [statute cited by Secretary of State].

**Exporting a record the system already holds is not creating a new record.** Section 84-712(3)(f) provides: "This section shall not be construed to require a public body or custodian of a public record to produce or generate any public record in a new or different form or format modified from that of the original public record." A custodian may not have to create a record that does not exist. A CVR, however, is not a new abstract prepared by hand. Election Systems & Software (ES&S), which supplies Nebraska's voting system, states in its public FAQ (current as of August 19, 2026) that "[a] cast vote record (CVR) is a record of the votes produced and recorded for each voter by the tabulation system. A CVR does not identify voters," and that "[m]odern versions of ES&S voting software provide the option to archive cast vote records, as well as ballot images." The same FAQ states that a jurisdiction "can opt to include these records when the ballot is programmed," and that "State rule, law or procedure dictate the management of CVR's and ballot images." If the system already produced that record, or can export it from data the system already holds, producing the export is not creating a new record.

## 4. The State's burden

The State has not shown that Nebraska's November 3, 2026 general election was free and fair. The burden to demonstrate the integrity of that election rests on the State. It has not met that burden while the electronic records of the count remain closed to the public.

## 5. Supporting context on the voting system

I raise the following only as background, not as the basis of this request. A 2020 Texas examination of ES&S EVS 6.1.1.0 reported that a vendor hash-verification script would report a successful result even when no reference hash file was present. See Brian Mechler, *Voting System Examination of Election Systems & Software EVS 6.1.1.0*, Texas Secretary of State, August 2020, §§ 7.2–7.3. Separately, Texas examiners identified an uncertified USB-stick software installation method on earlier ExpressVote software in the EVS 5.x and 6.0.x family. The U.S. Election Assistance Commission later approved a fix as Engineering Change Order ESS-1100 (October 14, 2020). I have not independently confirmed that the USB method applied to EVS 6.1.1.0, or which ES&S version Nebraska counties used in 2026.

## 6. Request

I respectfully ask your office to:

1. Open an investigation into county refusals to provide cast vote records, and into the integrity of the November 3, 2026 general election, in all 93 counties.
2. State publicly whether a CVR from that election, once it exists or can be exported from the tabulation system, is a public record under Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 84-712 to 84-712.09, and whether § 32-1032 or any other statute bars its release.
3. If you conclude that a CVR is a public record, coordinate with the Secretary of State and county election officials so that lawful requests are honored on the same basis statewide.

I would be grateful for a written response within 30 days.
